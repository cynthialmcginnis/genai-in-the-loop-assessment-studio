GenAI-in-the-Loop Assessment Studio

Turn an assignment you wrote before GenAI into one where students use GenAI with critical thinking. Upload your assignment exactly as students receive it, plus its learning outcomes. The studio shows where GenAI could now do the work, recommends a design, and rewrites your assignment so students use GenAI at planned steps, keep your outcomes, and are graded on their own judgment.

  • 1. Upload your assignment
  • 2. Add its outcomes
  • 3. Analyze
  • 4. Choose a design
  • 5. Get your redesigned assignment

Private by design. Everything runs in your browser. Nothing you paste or upload is sent anywhere or saved. Share only the assessment instructions and outcomes, never student work, names, or grades.

Your original assignment

Paste text, or upload a Word (.docx), PowerPoint (.pptx), PDF, or text file. For slides, the studio also reads the speaker notes, where instructions often live. Any kind of assignment works: essays, problem sets, labs, presentations, debates, role-plays, simulations, case studies, projects, discussion posts, code, and more.

No assignment handy? Preview the studio with a sample

What the studio sees

A starting point for your judgment, built from patterns in the text. It is not a measurement, and you know your course better than any rule does.

Your learning outcomes

The thinking each outcome asks for, based on its verb. These outcomes do not change in any design below.

Where GenAI could do the work now

Features in the assessment that make it easier or harder for GenAI to produce the work without the learning.

Recommended GenAI-in-the-loop designs

Ranked by how well each design fits your outcomes and your assessment. Every design keeps your outcomes, grades the student's judgment rather than GenAI's output, works in a fully online course, and has a route for students without GenAI. Open any design to see how it runs and copy its prompt.

Your redesigned assignment

Your assignment, rewritten so students use GenAI at planned steps and are graded on their own judgment. It starts with the top design; choose another design here or from any card above. Click into the assignment to edit it directly.

Student-facing assignment

The LMS file uses inline formatting, so it keeps its look when pasted into LMS editors that strip style sheets. Review every prompt and rubric weight before giving the assignment to students.

Where these designs come from. Several designs adapt published ideas: the AI Assignment Flip examples; Texas A&M University, Assignment Ideas; Michael Feldstein, How to ChatGPT-proof Analysis Assignments (e-Literate, 2023); the AI Pedagogy Project assignment collection; and UCLA HumTech, Designing Writing Assignments to Work with AI. Each design card names its source.

  Built for the Workshop-in-a-Box "Beyond AI Detection: Redesigning Authentic Assessments for the GenAI Era," by Molly Barra and Cynthia McGinnis, USM Generative AI Pedagogy Fellowship Program, 2026-2027. Draft. The analysis is rule-based and suggests designs; it does not make any assessment secure or prove who did the work.
